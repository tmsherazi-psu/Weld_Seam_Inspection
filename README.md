In automated weld inspection and industrial machine-vision systems, legacy industrial PCs often need to perform weld-seam image analysis under CPU-only and low-latency con- straints while annotated data remain limited. Under degraded imaging conditions, segmenta- tion masks may become broken or fragmented, which directly affects downstream geometric measurement and inspection reliability. To address this practical problem, we present a repro- ducible and interpretable image preprocessing and structural evaluation framework for weld- seam inspection on legacy industrial PCs. Four preprocessing paths were compared under identical deployment conditions: no preprocessing (Baseline), global histogram equalization (HE), gamma correction, and contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE). To better characterize weld-structure continuity, we introduced a Topology-Integrity Metric (TIM) that combines completeness and fragmentation. Statistical validation was conducted using paired nonparametric tests and interval estimation. Experimental results show that, under an approximate 45 ms per-frame latency constraint on a low-cost industrial CPU, CLAHE achieved the best structural continuity performance, improving TIM by 5.3 per- centage points (pp), with improved robustness after excluding extremely degraded samples. Analysis of outlier cases further indicates that, under extremely low SNR or strongly textured backgrounds, CLAHE may amplify non-weld structures and degrade structural evaluation. The proposed framework provides an engineering-oriented basis for selecting image prepro- cessing strategies in automated weld inspection under limited computational resources.
